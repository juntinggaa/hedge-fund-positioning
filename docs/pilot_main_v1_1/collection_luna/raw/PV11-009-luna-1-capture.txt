This CFTC positioning snapshot and model output indicate that leveraged funds hold a substantial net long position in 10-Year Treasury Note futures (+9.04% of open interest), but that stance is largely in line with model expectations rather than a statistically extreme or unexpectedly crowded trade.
1. Leveraged Funds: Moderately More Bullish Than Modeled, Within Normal Noise
Directional stance: At +355,518 contracts (+9.04% of open interest), leveraged funds (hedge funds, CTAs, and macro traders) are the primary net long force among reportable traders. In isolation, a net long futures position points toward expectations of higher 10-year note prices (lower yields)—or long-futures basis trades against short cash bonds.
Statistical significance of the residual: While actual net positioning (+9.04%) exceeds the model's point forecast (+7.31%) by +1.73 percentage points, it sits comfortably inside the 90% prediction interval (+3.74% to +10.88%). Because 39% of historical out-of-sample absolute residuals were at least as large (putting this residual near the 61st percentile of past errors, or less than one standard deviation under a normal approximation), the +1.73% surprise represents routine positioning noise rather than an abnormal structural shift or a crowded divergence from fundamentals.
2. Market Structure & Offsetting Counterparties
Asset Managers (High gross footprint, neutral net tilt): Asset managers dominate total market participation—holding 37.06% of open interest on the long side (1,458,078 contracts), 35.83% on the short side (1,409,497 contracts), and 20.04% in spreading (788,616 contracts)—yet their net exposure is nearly flat at +1.23% of open interest (+48,581 contracts). Institutional managers as a group are heavily active in duration management and curve/spread trades, but their bullish and bearish views largely offset one another in the aggregate.
Dealers (Flat net exposure): Dealers/intermediaries hold a negligible net short of -15,629 contracts (-0.40% of open interest), reflecting balanced book-running in the futures leg.

3. Key Analytical Caveat: Futures-Only vs. Cash-Futures Basis
Because this report covers futures only, net futures positioning does not translate one-for-one into outright duration exposure. Leveraged funds frequently execute cash-futures basis or relative-value trades (such as buying 10-year futures while shorting cash Treasuries or paying fixed in interest-rate swaps). Consequently, the +9.04% net long futures position could reflect a mix of outright bullish duration bets (positioning for lower yields) and relative-value arbitrage where futures are cheap relative to cash bonds.
