The CFTC data and model output show a 10-Year U.S. Treasury Note futures market where gross liquidity is anchored by institutional asset managers, net directional long exposure is concentrated in leveraged funds, and offsetting net short exposure is held by "Other Reportable" and non-reportable (smaller) traders—with leveraged-fund positioning tracking model expectations almost exactly.
1. Structural Positioning Across Trader Categories
Leveraged Funds Hold the Dominant Net Long (+9.04% of OI): Hedge funds, CTAs, and macro traders hold 853,740 long contracts against 498,222 short contracts (a 1.71-to-1 ratio), creating a net long of 355,518 contracts. Within this futures contract alone, they are the primary holders of net long duration.
Asset Managers Dominate Gross Volume but Are Nearly Balanced Net (+1.23% of OI): Institutional investors account for the majority of market participation—holding 57.1% of total open interest on the long side and 55.9% on the short side once their 788,616 spreading contracts (20.0% of total OI) are included. Despite that massive footprint, their directional longs (1,458,078) and shorts (1,409,497) nearly offset each other, indicating deeply divided institutional views or balanced hedging across funds.
Dealers Are Flat Intermediaries (-0.40% of OI): Dealer/Intermediary accounts hold a relatively small gross footprint (roughly 5.4% to 5.8% of directional OI) and a negligible net short of -15,629 contracts, consistent with warehousing transient client flows rather than taking directional bets.
Other Reportables and Implied Non-Reportables Supply the Short Side (-9.87% of OI Combined): Because every futures contract has both a long and a short, total market net positioning must sum to zero. Across the four reportable categories, net positioning sums to +229,381 contracts (+5.83% of OI):
Reportable totals (including 939,201 spreading contracts on both sides): 3,596,873 total reportable longs (91.42% of OI) and 3,367,492 total reportable shorts (85.59% of OI).
Implied Non-Reportable (small trader) positions: Subtracting reportable totals from total open interest (3,934,302) leaves non-reportable traders with 337,429 longs (8.58% of OI) and 566,810 shorts (14.41% of OI), for a net short of -229,381 contracts (-5.83% of OI).
Combined with Other Reportables (such as corporate treasuries, proprietary trading firms, or smaller institutions hedging rate lockouts at -159,089 net contracts, or -4.04% of OI), these two groups absorb almost all of the net long exposure held by leveraged funds and asset managers.
2. What the Supervised Model Output Tells Us
No Positioning Shock or Anomaly: While a +9.04% net long by leveraged funds looks large in isolation, the model predicted +9.23% of OI (with a 90% prediction interval of +5.66% to +12.79%).
High Conditional Predictability: The residual of -0.19 percentage points is smaller in magnitude than 91% of historical out-of-sample errors. Whatever variables drive the model (such as prior positioning persistence, yield momentum, curve slope, or cross-category flows), leveraged funds are behaving right in line with historical relationships rather than staging an unexpected squeeze, capitulation, or regime shift.
Wide Uncertainty Bounds Around Fair Value: The 90% out-of-sample error band spans 7.13 percentage points of open interest (roughly 280,500 contracts on either side of the error distribution), showing that any residual within a few tenths of a percent is pure noise.
3. Key Caveats in Interpreting Futures-Only Data
Unobserved Cash-Futures Basis and Curve Trades: A net long in 10-Year Treasury futures does not prove leveraged funds are net bullish on Treasury prices overall. Hedge funds frequently pair long 10-Year futures against short cash Treasuries (a reverse cash-futures basis trade), short interest-rate swaps, or short positions in adjacent futures tenors (such as 2-Year, 5-Year, or Ultra 10-Year/30-Year Bond futures in yield-curve steepener or butterfly trades).
Single-Week Snapshot vs. Flow Momentum: Without the prior week's levels or historical unconditional percentiles, this snapshot shows who holds the contracts on September 21, 2021, but cannot distinguish whether leveraged funds were actively adding to longs or paring back an even larger prior position.
