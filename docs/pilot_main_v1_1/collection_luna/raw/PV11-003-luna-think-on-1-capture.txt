3. The model indicates that the position is largely expected
The model expected leveraged funds to be net long by 9.23% of open interest, with an approximate 90% range of +5.66% to +12.79%.

The actual reading was +9.04%, producing a residual of just -0.19 percentage points.

That has two implications:

The positioning is consistent with the model's bullish expectation. Actual exposure is close to the predicted level and comfortably within the model's approximate range.

The observation is not a meaningful surprise. Only about 9% of earlier absolute out-of-sample residuals were smaller. In other words, the model's prediction was unusually accurate this week.

The small residual should not be confused with a small position. Leveraged funds still had a substantial net long exposure; it was simply very close to what the model anticipated.

4. What this might mean for the Treasury market
The most natural interpretation is that leveraged funds had a significant bullish exposure to 10-Year Treasury futures, potentially reflecting expectations of lower yields, demand for duration, or relative-value opportunities.

There are important qualifications:

Positioning is not necessarily outright directional conviction. Leveraged funds can use Treasury futures in relative-value, spread, and other hedging strategies. The report does not reveal the purpose of each position.

A large net long can create vulnerability to liquidation. If yields rise and leveraged funds reduce their exposure, selling pressure in futures could amplify a Treasury-market decline.

The data are a snapshot, not a forecast. They do not establish whether the positions are increasing or decreasing without comparison to previous weeks.

Overall assessment
The September 21, 2021 positioning suggests a meaningful bullish bias in 10-Year Treasury futures, concentrated among leveraged funds, alongside sizeable net short exposure from Other Reportable traders.

The model reinforces that interpretation: the leveraged-fund position was almost exactly what it predicted, so the data do not indicate an unexpected positioning shock. The more interesting market implication is the potential asymmetry: a large leveraged long position may support Treasury prices while confidence remains intact, but could also create downside pressure if those positions are unwound.

Bottom line: The positioning is bullish in directional terms, but not an unambiguous bullish market signal. It indicates substantial leveraged-fund exposure and potential vulnerability to a reversal, rather than proving that Treasury yields are about to fall.
