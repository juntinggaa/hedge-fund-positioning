* **Actual vs. Expected:** The model estimated an expected net position of **+7.31% of open interest**, driven by variables such as yields, curve shape, volatility, funding stress, and lagged positions.
* **Modest Positive Residual:** The actual net position (+9.04%) exceeded the model's expectation by **+1.73 percentage points** of open interest.
* **Historical Normality:** Despite this positive residual, it is not an extreme statistical anomaly. Approximately 61% of earlier absolute out-of-sample residuals were smaller, meaning a 1.73-percentage-point gap happens fairly often. Furthermore, the actual position fell comfortably within the model's approximate 90% error range (+3.74% to +10.88%). This indicates that leveraged fund positioning was slightly more net long than prevailing macroeconomic and market factors strictly implied, but still well within normal historical bounds.

### 3. What the Data Does *Not* Tell Us (Important Caveats)

* **Motives Remain Unidentified:** As noted in the model's framework, a net long futures position does not explicitly prove outright directional bullishness. It can coexist with complex relative-value strategies, such as basis trades. However, because the cash leg is unobserved and the model inputs do not directly measure cash-futures basis profitability, the exact strategy mix cannot be deduced.
* **Not a Predictive Forecast:** Because the model incorporates current-week open interest data, this output reflects a retrospective conditional snapshot rather than a pre-Friday nowcast or a forward-looking directional signal for future weeks.
