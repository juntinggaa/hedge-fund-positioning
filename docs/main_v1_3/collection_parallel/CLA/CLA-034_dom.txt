      - list:
        - strong: Asset managers’ long
        - text: mostly reflects demand for duration. Pension funds, insurers, bond funds and benchmarked managers often get Treasury exposure through futures because it ties up less balance sheet and cash than buying the bonds.         - strong: Leveraged funds’ short
        - text: is largely the other side of that demand. Hedge funds typically sell the futures that asset managers want and buy the underlying cash Treasuries, financed in repo. This is the cash-futures basis trade. Their short is therefore mostly hedged and should not be read as a bet that yields will rise. A common mistake is to call this a “crowded short” that sets up a squeeze. That logic doesn’t really apply when the shorts are paired with cash longs.
        - strong: Dealers’ net short
        - text: fits their role as intermediaries. They hedge inventory and absorb flows that don’t net out between the other two groups.
