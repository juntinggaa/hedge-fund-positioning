      - heading "What each group is likely doing" [level=3]
      - paragraph:
        - strong: Asset managers (+3.0M net, 51% of OI).
        - text: Pension funds, insurers, bond mutual funds and similar investors use futures to get duration exposure. Futures are capital-efficient and need little cash upfront, which leaves the rest of the portfolio for credit or other assets. This is a structural demand for duration, not a short-term call on rates. The steady demand tends to make futures trade slightly rich relative to cash Treasuries.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: Leveraged funds (−2.23M net, −38% of OI).
        - text: Hedge funds and relative-value funds capture that richness. They buy the cheapest-to-deliver cash notes, finance them in repo, and sell futures against them, earning the small spread with heavy leverage. The gross short of about 2.73M contracts, against roughly 2.23M net, is about $220bn+ of face value in this contract alone. Some of the short is directional, for example trend-following CTAs, which the TFF report also classes as leveraged funds. The mirror image of the asset-manager long, however, points to the basis trade.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: Dealers (−630k net, −11% of OI).
        - text: Dealers mainly intermediate and hedge. A net futures short often offsets long cash inventory, which has tended to be heavy as Treasury issuance has stayed large. It is not a strong directional signal either.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: Other reportables (−166k net, −3% of OI).
        - text: This group is small relative to the others and doesn’t change the picture.
